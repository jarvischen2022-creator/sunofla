AFTER A PILOT

There are three practical paths — not one default package.

The Pilot shows what the workflow actually needs. The next step may be cloud-only, local or hybrid controls, or additional continuity infrastructure. Hardware is one possible outcome — not the starting assumption.

PATH 01

Continue in the cloud

Keep the proven workflow in a dedicated cloud workspace when it remains the simplest practical option for the team.

Best when one workflow, a small team, and approved cloud use are enough.

PATH 02

Add local or hybrid controls

Keep selected files, workflows, or AI capability closer to the office while using cloud models only where they add value.

Consider when file size, data location, shared access, or workflow needs make local controls useful.

PATH 03

Add continuity infrastructure

Add shared storage, protected backup, and recovery planning when one device or one failed drive can disrupt the team's work.

Consider when shared project data, recovery needs, or delivery schedules make continuity important.

A Pilot can also show that the existing tools are enough for now. Expanding is a decision — not an obligation.

WHAT STAYS UNDER YOUR CONTROL

The recommendation follows your boundaries — not a hardware sales target.

Before a broader deployment, the team should know what information is connected, who can use it, which AI routes are allowed, and what still needs a person to decide.

Approved sources

Only the files, procedures, and templates chosen for the workflow are connected.

Roles and access

The team decides who can use which sources and actions.

Cloud and local boundaries

Cloud use, local handling, and human review points are chosen by workflow.

Visible cost and review effort

Model usage, exceptions, and the human time needed to check results remain part of the decision.

Written scope before equipment

Hardware, installation, and support are quoted separately in writing only after a practical recommendation.

WHEN LOCAL ADDS REAL VALUE

Local systems are useful when the work needs them.

A local foundation is not a replacement for every cloud tool. It becomes useful when the team needs faster access to large files, clearer control over shared sources, dependable recovery, or selected local AI capability.

Local AI compute

Useful for selected internal workflows, retrieval, transcription, or automation that benefit from local capability.

Shared storage

Useful when a team needs one permissioned, recoverable place for active project files and approved sources.

Backup and recovery

Useful when a failed drive, accidental deletion, or interrupted service can affect active work.

A PC handles AI work. Shared storage protects and shares files. Cloud remains useful for selected complex or occasional tasks.

PILOT EXAMPLES

Start with work the team already repeats.

A Pilot does not begin with a prompt library. It begins with one repeatable task, approved inputs, a responsible reviewer, and a clear place for the result.

01

Project intake and client update

Turn approved meeting notes, messages, and project files into a draft summary, action list, and client update. A project lead reviews before anything is saved or sent.

02

Proposal preparation

Use approved templates and intake information to prepare a first draft and identify missing scope, timing, or assumptions. A responsible person verifies pricing, commitments, and terms before sending.

03

Internal knowledge search

Search only approved procedures, product information, and past templates. Answers point to their source, and uncertain items are sent to a person for review.
